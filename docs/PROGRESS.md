- 017 2026-09-08 startlat-20 — the start latency now fits inside one latch interval: `CAL STARTLAT` 20 µs and `CAL TRIGCOMP` 2 µs, persisted, delivering the first output step **20.26 µs (sd 41 ns)** after the physical edge — cashing in phase 14's pre-arm, which had never been re-measured on silicon, and phase 16's hardware delay. `bench_arm.py`'s warmed column is **5.92–6.00 µs for every shape**, flat in stage count, waveform type and whether the train is measured, which is the first silicon evidence that moving the preparation into `loop()` did what it claimed (the phase-13 figures it replaces ran 9.0 to 15.0 µs); the cold path is unchanged. The arithmetic floor `arm + PRELOAD + DACPROG2 + 3 + TRIGCOMP` comes to exactly 20, but the firmware's own verdict under real trigger edges breaks between 16 and 18 — at 16 it asks for 17 — so 20 carries 3 µs of real margin on a joint route. **The cost is that an independent `TRIG` route no longer fits**: it arms two engines from one edge and wants 30–35 µs, so a bench routing two slots independently from one input must raise `STARTLAT` back and give up the sample-aligned latency; a single train driving both channels keeps both, and was already the right construct because independent routes contend for their first latch by ~10 µs. Not a silent failure — the train runs and its completion names the value that would have covered it. `startlat_trig.py` now covers both route modes, which took two fixes: the independent case has to be `S` and not `L` (two `L` ramps latch every ~10 µs between them and buried the arm's verdict under ~2470 "already due" notes per train, measuring dual-engine throughput instead), and the verdict has to separate the arm's `WARN` from the throughput `#` note, which two busy engines produce at any `STARTLAT`. Compensation verified: `t0` lands at 18 µs, the hardware adds 2.26 µs — the same figure measured against a 35 µs budget, so it does not depend on the budget — and the leftover 0.26 µs is just `CAL` taking whole microseconds. A triggered start now leads a `T`/`U` start by ~2 µs, since two of `TRIGCOMP`'s three terms are common to both paths; that is the accepted trade for an absolute edge-to-output latency. Compiled defaults stay 35/0 on purpose — `TRIGCOMP` is a property of the board, not a constant to copy — so only this board's EEPROM carries the new values (`P` does persist `CAL` at `TrainStore.cpp:548`, though its reply does not say so). Two harness bugs fixed: `sjcon.ASYNC` never learned about phase 15's `MRANGE` or `WARN engine:`, so a train completing mid-command derailed the next `cmd1`; and `make_bench_figures.py` hardcoded `CAL_STARTLAT = 35`, so `trigcomp.py` now writes both budgets into every CSV row and the figures read them (progress/017-startlat-20.md)
- 016 2026-09-08 config-b-trigcomp — the last unmeasured quantity in the firmware has a number: the output leaves its baseline 37.27 µs (sd 49 ns over 30 shots) after the physical edge at IN0 crosses the level that pin actually switches at, so against the 35 µs `CAL STARTLAT` the unaccounted hardware delay — what `CAL TRIGCOMP` would absorb — is 2.27 µs rather than the few hundred nanoseconds predicted, because a scope on the trigger input and one output sees the pin-to-ISR delay, the PIT wake with its 0.8 µs quantum and the AD5752's latch-to-output delay only summed, and `NLDAC` is not brought out to separate them; `TRIGCOMP` is left at **0** on purpose, since it is subtracted on the trigger path alone while two of its three terms apply to a `T`/`U` start too, so applying it would make triggered trains lead software-started ones by ~2 µs — a decision about what the instrument guarantees, not a calibration. Getting a trustworthy number meant correcting the prescribed procedure twice: reference the edge to the pin's own measured threshold (1.757 ± 0.022 V, from walking the AWG peak down until trains stop) and not to half the AWG's amplitude, because the edge slews only 0.75 V/µs and the choice moves the answer 1.7 µs; and time the *foot* of the output step and not its 50 % point, which is contaminated by settling — the foot agrees to six nanoseconds between an 8 V and a 2 V step, which is the control that says the load is not in the answer. Two faults surfaced on the way, neither related to the measurement: the channel-0 **current readback is 4.02× low in voltage mode** (a 1 kΩ resistor reads 3988 Ω through the board's own V/I, but 993 Ω when current mode forces the current and the scope reads the voltage, and the board's voltage readback agrees with the scope throughout — so every voltage-mode `MSUM`/`MDATA`/`READ` current is a quarter of the truth and phase 015's OLED load resistances are four times it, which is now the top open item); and `pico2000.py` had been multiplying `get_timebase`'s nanosecond interval by its `time_units` code, which is the unit of a different call's timestamp array, so timebases 1–5 reported intervals a thousand times too small and `pick_timebase` had never chosen one — every capture in the repo was taken at 640 ns when 20 ns was free (fixed; no existing result changes, because `capture.py` only ever asks for 640 ns or coarser). A false alarm is recorded too: timebases 0 and 1 *looked* like they sampled slower than they claimed, by a clean 1/3 and 3/4, until de-glitching the edge counting showed it was noise dithering across the threshold on a slow edge — every timebase the driver accepts delivers what it claims. Also measured: the analog output reaches 90 % in 1.91 µs while `BENCHSETTLE` says 8–9 µs, so `CAL SETTLE` is mostly the ADC path; and the voltage-mode output runs 10–15 % below command, worsening with load current. New `tests/device/trigcomp.py` (check / threshold / measure) and `tests/device/scope_timebase.py`, four explanatory figures from `docs/figures/make_bench_figures.py` (progress/016-config-b-trigcomp.md)
- 015 2026-09-07 ui-results-clock — the box now runs headless and says so (the OLED library never probes the bus, so an unplugged panel used to cost up to 340 ms of blocked `loop()` per frame and starve the MDATA drain; `UiMenu` probes it itself and gates only the frame push, so `SCREEN` still works with nothing connected), Btn1/Btn2 fire the two trigger routes again through a loop()-context `Triggers::fireRoute` with arm/re-arm debounce (one press is one train whatever the contact does) while Btn0 walks a flat page list — STATUS, up to four RESULT pages of the last train's per-point V, I and load resistance per channel, SYSTEM — also reachable by `PAGE`; resistance is printed to the precision the two standard errors support (floored at 1 % for the uncharacterised ADC gain, capped at three significant figures, with `open` and `--` where a number would lie) and readings at the driver's 9 V ceiling or the pump's 3 mA limit are marked `*` — from the *extremes* of the train, because `Accum` now also keeps min/max (two ISR comparisons, 8 bytes) and reports them as a new `MRANGE` record beside `MSUM`, one excursion in five hundred being exactly what a mean hides;
